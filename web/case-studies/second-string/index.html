Case study · Second String

Designing sub plans: help for the games you can't watch

Second String is a fantasy football app I designed and built. Its headline feature lets players line up backups ahead of time, so an injury mid-game doesn't cost them the week.

Role
Product designer and engineer, solo
Platforms
Web and mobile app
Work
Flows, wireframes, UI, front and back end
Status
Live at secondstring.app

The problem

Fantasy players set a lineup before the games start, then life happens. If a starter is ruled out halfway through a game, that spot stops scoring, and in most apps the only fix is to be on your phone at the right moment.

The feature had to work while the player isn't looking, so the rules had to be ones people could trust without watching:

  • Only a real injury triggers a swap. A player marked OUT, never a coach's decision or a hunch.
  • No going back. Once a starter is swapped out, they're locked out for that week.
  • Fair scoring. One lineup spot can't earn more than one game's worth of points.

The user flow

Two moments matter: a minute of setup before kickoff, and the game-day swap the player never has to touch.

On a phone, swipe sideways to see the whole flow, or open it full size.

Wireframes

Four phone screens take a player through the whole loop, from noticing an unprotected spot to seeing the swap happen.

Design decisions

Prompt, don't hide

A feature people never find doesn't help them. The league home names how many starters are unprotected, so setup starts from the page players open every week.

Limit the choices

Three backups per starter, and only players who can legally fill that spot. Fewer options keep the screen quick to finish and rule out plans that could never work.

Explain the rules once, in place

The two rules that surprise people (only OUT triggers a swap, and there's no coming back) appear on the screen where the plan is made, not in a help page.

Make automation visible

An app acting on its own has to show its work. The game-day alert and the highlighted swap explain a score change before anyone has to ask.
